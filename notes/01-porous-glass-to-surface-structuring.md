# Porous-glass sealing and passivation

> Surface pore closure in porous glass while preserving the porous bulk.

The 2015 passivation study described the process as thermal densification using a graphite target [1]. In the wider research line, the same glass–graphite geometry was also described using the terms laser-induced black-body heating and, later, laser-induced microplasma processing [2].

## Motivation

Porous glass contains a connected nanoscale pore network that can host liquids, molecules and other functional species. The same open structure also interacts with the environment and can change during storage.

The aim of the experiment was to modify the outer surface sufficiently to reduce access to the pore network while preserving the porous material below it.

The porous-glass sample was placed in tight contact with compacted graphite. A pulsed ytterbium-fiber laser passed through the glass and was absorbed by the graphite, producing a localized high-temperature interaction at the interface. The published study used a wavelength of about **1.06 µm**, a pulse duration of **100 ns** and a repetition rate of **30–50 kHz** [1].

## Surface modification and passivation

Atomic-force microscopy showed collapse of the open porous morphology at the treated surface, consistent with pore closure and local densification. Cross-sectional observations showed that porous material remained below the treated layer [1].

Immediately after treatment, optical transmission decreased by less than about **5–10%** across the measured spectral range. After this initial change, treated samples stored in laboratory air at relative humidity above **70%** showed no significant further decrease in transmission over **10 months**, while untreated porous glass continued to change [1].

Graphite deposited during processing was removed by wet laser cleaning in distilled water on the same laser system, without a noticeable additional transmission loss [1]. A later study compared wet laser cleaning with thermal and chemical cleaning methods [3].

The reported results therefore support surface pore closure with retention of the porous bulk and improved optical stability during storage.

## Historical context

Related results were presented at the 15th International Symposium on Laser Precision Microfabrication in Vilnius in 2014, including work on porous-glass densification and LIBBH-based surface stabilization [4,5]. The journal papers followed in 2015.
